# FFB effect equalizer — 10 bands (fw ≥ 1.2.10.10)

The 2026-07 PitHouse build expands the wheelbase "FFB Effect Equalizer" from
6 to 10 frequency bands and raises the slider range. Wire-verified live on
2026-07-31 against an R25 base on firmware **1.2.10.10** (version reply
`84 21 01 02 0A 0A` in the same capture) by stepping every slider one at a
time through the real-wheel bridge
(`sim/logs/bridge-20260731-064830.jsonl`).

Four **new command IDs** appear — `0x32 0x33 0x34 0x35` — on the standard
wheelbase settings groups (write `0x29`, read `0x28`, dev `0x13`), same
shape as every other `base-*` setting: 1-byte cmdid + 2-byte big-endian
int.

Each slider was moved individually (0 → 500 %, the 100 Hz band 0 → 100 %)
and the changed register identified per burst:

The four new bands are **interleaved** into the ladder (10, 30, 50,
80 Hz), not appended above it. New-band cmd order `32..35` happens to
also be frequency order among the new four.

> **Old-UI relabel (user-confirmed 2026-07-31).** The pre-update 6-band
> UI labeled the bands 10/15/25/40/60/100 Hz (cf. the plugin's
> `MozaData.cs` comment, "0-400%"). The update relabeled the lowest
> band: `0x0E` now carries the **5 Hz** label and the new `0x32` takes
> **10 Hz** — the register↔frequency binding shifted for band 1.
> Consumers that treated `equalizer1` as "the 10 Hz band" (e.g. the
> plugin's `Equalizer1` property) now point at 5 Hz in UI terms. Whether
> the firmware's underlying filter center moved, or only the label, is
> not observable on the wire.

## Value encoding & range

Raw percent as BE u16 — no scaling: 500 % → `0x01F4`, 100 % → `0x64`,
90 % → `0x5A`, 150 % → `0x96`.

- Bands 5–80 Hz: UI slider **0–500 %** (was 0–100 % pre-update).
- Band 100 Hz (`2C`): UI slider stays **0–100 %**.

The wire encoding is u16 throughout, so the caps are PitHouse-side clamps;
firmware acceptance above 500 was not probed.

## Observed PitHouse behaviour

- **Whole-bank rewrite:** any single slider change re-emits all 10
  registers in one ~50 ms burst (old IDs first — `0E 0F 10 11 14 2C` —
  then `32 33 34 35`, ordering within the burst varies slightly).
- **ACKs:** the base ACKs each write on group `0xA9` (dev nibble-swapped
  `0x31`) echoing cmd + value — including the new `0x32..0x35`, so fw
  1.2.10.10 genuinely stores all 10 (confirmed by read-back, below).
- **Read sweep:** the settings-page open sweeps reads over group `0x28`
  including the new IDs; base replies on `0xA8` with stored values
  (e.g. `32 00 5A` = 90).
- **Profile-wins quirk:** the page-open read returned `0E 00 5A` (eq1 = 90
  in base EEPROM) yet the UI showed 0 and the first apply burst wrote
  `0E 00 00` — PitHouse treats its local profile as source of truth and
  overwrites the base's stored values on apply, rather than adopting the
  read-back.

## Sensitivity presets (0–10)

The same PitHouse build adds eleven "sensitivity settings" (UI 0–10) on
the EQ page. Each is a **compound preset**: one selection writes
`road-sensitivity` (cmd `0x0C`) plus a canned curve into all 10 EQ
registers — 11 writes on group `0x29`, no other registers touched. All
eleven were captured by clicking each in turn (same capture,
t=1785510957…1785511380).

`road-sensitivity` follows the affine map **`0x0C` = 10 + 4×N** (UI 0 →
10 … UI 10 → 50), exact across all 11 points. The EQ curves open
progressively from a low-pass shelf to fully flat:

| UI | `0C` | 5 | 10 | 15 | 25 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 | 80 | 100 Hz |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 10 | 100 | 100 | 30 | 10 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 14 | 100 | 100 | 60 | 20 | 10 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 18 | 100 | 100 | 70 | 40 | 30 | 10 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 3 | 22 | 100 | 100 | 80 | 50 | 40 | 20 | 10 | 10 | 0 | 0 |
| 4 | 26 | 100 | 100 | 90 | 60 | 50 | 30 | 20 | 20 | 10 | 0 |
| 5 | 30 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 70 | 60 | 40 | 30 | 30 | 10 | 0 |
| 6 | 34 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 90 | 80 | 50 | 40 | 40 | 20 | 0 |
| 7 | 38 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 90 | 60 | 60 | 60 | 40 | 0 |
| 8 | 42 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 90 | 80 | 80 | 80 | 60 | 0 |
| 9 | 46 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 80 | 0 |
| 10 | 50 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 |

Values are the usual raw-percent BE u16. The 100 Hz band stays 0 for
every preset except 10, where the whole bank goes flat at 100. The
presets are one-shot macros — PitHouse holds no separate "sensitivity"
register; re-reading after a preset returns the individual `0x0C` + EQ
values it wrote. Within a burst PitHouse may re-emit some registers
twice with identical values (also observed on plain slider changes).

The selector has one more position beyond the 11 numbered presets: a
**"customized" state** shown when the current values don't match any
preset row. It is **purely PitHouse-derived UI — nothing on the wire**:
in this capture the selector sat on it for ~75 min (after the manual
per-band slider walk) with zero group-`0x29` writes. Notably `0x0C`
read 50 at the time — identical to preset 10's value — while the EQ
bank held hand-tuned values, and the UI still showed "customized": the
match is computed over the **full tuple** (road-sensitivity + all 10 EQ
registers), not `0x0C` alone.
